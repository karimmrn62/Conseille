Research date: 20 Nov 2026. Method: web search (US index). ## 1. Market size: global management consulting, strategy segment, France, UK, US

### Takeaway
Market size depends heavily on definition: Source Global Research (the most cited "professional-firm" view) puts global consulting at ~$275bn in 2025 (+6%), with strategy consulting ~$60bn+. France (Syntec Conseil / Consult'in France) fell 1.5% in 2025 with +2% expected in 2026; UK (MCA) ~£21.8bn in 2025 (+3%); the US is the largest market and was forecast at ~6-7% growth. The mood is "slow growth + AI disruption of the billable-hour model".

### Cited Findings
**Global**
- Source Global Research: global consulting market to reach $275bn in 2025, +6% on a "disappointing" 2024 — [Source Global Research](https://www.sourceglobalresearch.com/?p=131220) (snippet only; methodology not checked).
- Source: strategy consulting is the second-largest service line after technology & innovation and "set to edge past $60bn for the first time in 2025" (global) — [Source Global Research, US Consulting Market 2025 extract](https://reports.sourceglobalresearch.com/report/download/9588/extract/The+US+Consulting+Market+in+2025) (snippet).

## 2. Independent / freelance management consultants and the platforms serving them

### Takeaway
Independents are a large, growing pool: ~150k self-employed management analysts in the US (inferred from BLS), ~30% of UK consultants self-employed, ~72k independent consultants in France. Platforms (Malt Strategy/Comatch, Catalant, BTG, Umbrex, Eden McCallum, Graphite) have networks of ~10k-100k+; they use AI mainly for matching, not for delivering advice — no platform found offering a consultant-facing strategic AI copilot.

### Cited Findings
**Counts**
- US: management analysts held 1,075,100 jobs in 2024; projected +9% 2024-34 (+94,500); median wage $101,190 (May 2024); "self-employed workers" are the second-largest employer category at 14% (after professional, scientific & technical services 34%) — [BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/management-analysts.htm).
- Vault: >1 million management consultants and analysts in the US, ~14.4% self-employed (undated) — [Vault](https://legacy.vault.com/industries-professions/industries/consulting/the-industry-today).
- US independent workforce overall: 72.9m independent workers (MBO Partners, all occupations) — [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/number-of-independent-workers-rises-again-mbo-partners).
- UK: >31% of management consultants and business analysts self-employed (undated) — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/hospital-management-administration/the-rise-of-the-freelance-consultant-7-things-to-know/); 2017: nearly a third of 175,000 UK management & digital consultants were freelancers — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/consulting/freelance/page/3) (OLD DATA). IBISWorld: 180,351 management consulting businesses in the UK in 2025 — [IBISWorld](https://img2.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/number-of-businesses/management-consulting/3910/).
- Eden McCallum 2025 UK survey: freelance consulting is one of the fastest-growing segments of the UK industry; independents satisfied with freedom/work-life — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/39583/independents-consultants-satisfied-with-their-work-life-and-career-freedom).
- France: 72,025 independent consultants vs 227,756 salaried consulting employees (year unspecified) — [Propulse by CA](https://propulsebyca.fr/idees-business/metiers-du-conseil/marche) (secondary). Total French freelancers ~1.3m (all sectors) — [tool-advisor](https://tool-advisor.fr/blog/chiffres-freelance/) (secondary).

**Platforms**
- Malt acquired Comatch (March 2022) and rebranded it Malt Strategy — [Consultor](https://www.consultor.fr/articles/malt-achete-comatch-trois-ex-mac-aux-commandes). Comatch network cited at >15,000 consultants when folded into Malt; an older figure says 9,000+ — [Consultancy.eu](https://www.consultancy.eu/firms/comatch/news/consulting/milestones) (dates unclear).
- Malt UK community reached 15,000 freelancers (June 2023) — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/34588/malt-uk-freelance-community-booms-to-15000-freelancers) (OLD).
- Malt launched "AI Search" (Nov 2024) using external LLMs + in-house small models for freelancer selection; a 2025 product update says AI Search auto-classifies projects including Malt Strategy ones — [itdaily](https://itdaily.com/blogs/software/ai-connects-companies-freelancers); [Malt UK product pulse 2025](https://www.malt.uk/resources/article/product-pulse-key-features-in-2025). Malt also introduced AI to help clients assemble consultant teams — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/38796/malt-introduces-ai-to-help-clients-assemble-better-consultant-teams).
- Malt Strategy rates: no published averages; daily rate set per consultant; platform fee reportedly 5-10% (AI-generated Dealroom text, low confidence) — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/malt_strategy).
- Catalant: network reported variously at 50,000+, 70,000+ and 100,000+ independent consultants plus ~1,000 boutique firms (secondary, inconsistent); a third-party profile reports a Sept 2026 launch of "Forward Deployed Experts" pairing operators with AI engineers (unverified); Catalant also publishes on GenAI empowering independents — [gtm8020 review](https://www.gtm8020.com/blog/catalant-review); [yespress](https://yespress.io/catalant); [Catalant](https://catalant.com/digital-and-ai/how-genai-is-reshaping-the-industry-and-empowering-independent-consultants/).
- Graphite (12,000+ vetted experts) reportedly acquired (by Catalant per comparison piece; year not stated) — [gtm8020](https://www.gtm8020.com/blog/catalant-review) (unverified).

**Rates (France)**
- Median TJM for management consulting freelancers €580/day, senior strategy/digital transformation >€750/day (attributed to "Baromètre Malt 2026" by an affiliate site — low confidence) — [freelance-solution.fr](https://www.freelance-solution.fr/?p=2779).

### Inferences
- US self-employed management analysts ≈ 14% × 1.075m ≈ ~150k (inference from BLS/Vault, not a published count). UK ≈ 50-60k self-employed (31% of ~175-180k; dated). France ≈ 70k. → Core English-speaking + France independent pool ≈ 250-300k, plus the rest of EU/world.
- At a €580-750/day TJM, an independent bills ~€100-150k/yr; a €30-100/month tool is <1% of revenue — price is not the barrier, trust/quality is.
- Platforms monetise matching/transactions; an AI advisor could be a partner/distribution channel (white-label or perk for network members) rather than a competitor — no evidence of a platform shipping a delivery-side strategy copilot.

- Malt Strategy current network size and rates not found.

## 4. AI adoption among consultants and executives (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Gen-AI use is near-universal among consultants (72-90% daily depending on survey) and AI is the #1 CEO investment priority (71% KPMG 2025), but enterprise impact is still early (McKinsey: 88% use AI somewhere, only 23% scaling agents). The top concern is accuracy/hallucination; consulting clients are also wary of AI-produced deliverables.

### Cited Findings
**Consultants**
- LexisNexis "Setting the Pace" (2025, vendor-commissioned): ~80% daily use per LexisNexis blogs; Consultancy.uk (Jan 2026) reports 90% of management consultants use Gen AI in daily tasks; 56% say they save 3-4 hours a day — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/42760/90-of-management-consultants-use-gen-ai-in-their-daily-tasks); [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/42670/five-ways-gen-ai-is-boosting-day-to-day-management-consulting-work); [LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/blogs/amppage/20/ai-in-management-consulting).
- EM Lyon "Consulting AI Barometer" (anonymous survey of ~100 consultants, Oct 2025-Jan 2026, France): 72% use AI every day; main uses: research, synthesis, idea structuring, deliverable preparation, drafting — [EM Lyon](https://em-lyon.com/en/press-releases/consulting-ai-barometer) (snippet; full page not accessible).
- Half of consultants cite misinformation (hallucinations) as their main concern, yet unofficial ("shadow") tool use is widespread — [Business Reporter](https://www.business-reporter.co.uk/technology/consultings-risky-gap-between-ai-confidence-and-control).

**Consulting clients (Source Global Research)**
- 81% of clients paid for consulting support on AI in the past 12 months; 99% expect to use AI advisors in future; >90% think AI will change the consulting process (70% significantly); 17% would prefer to deal with an agentic AI rather than a human — [Source](https://sourceglobalresearch.com/blog-post/understanding-the-market-for-ai-related-consulting-services) (undated, possibly 2024-25).
- Gen AI use "largely experimental", fewer than 1 in 5 companies using it extensively — [Source extract: Client attitudes to AI](https://reports.sourceglobalresearch.com/report/download/9597/extract/Client+attitudes+to+AI+and+barriers+to+implementation+today) (date unclear).
- Aug 2026: consultancy clients distrust AI-generated reports; "showdown" between consultants and clients over AI-driven fee pressure — [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-08-04-consultancy-clients-distrust-ai-reports/); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/08/31/consultants-head-for-a-showdown-with-their-own-clients/).
- Excitement for GenAI remains but revenue impact for consulting firms minimal — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/43279/excitement-remains-for-genai-but-revenue-impact-remains-minimal).

**Executives / enterprises**
- McKinsey State of AI 2025 (survey 25 Jun-29 Jul 2025, n=1,993): 88% regular AI use in ≥1 function (78% a year earlier); 62% at least experimenting with AI agents; 23% scaling agents somewhere; ≤10% scaling agents in any given function — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025).
- KPMG 2025 CEO Outlook (n=1,350 CEOs): 71% say AI is a top investment priority (64% prior year; another KPMG page says 52% in 2024); 69% allocate 10-20% of budget to AI; 67% expect ROI in 1-3 years; US 74%, Canada 78% — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/fi/en/insights/business-transformation/kpmg-2025-global-ceo-outlook.html); [KPMG Canada](https://kpmg.com/ca/en/home/media/press-releases/2025/10/canadian-ceos-focus-on-boosting-productivity-through-ai.print.html); [Accounting Times](https://www.accountingtimes.com.au/technology/kpmg-survey-reveals-ceos-plan-to-bolster-ai-investment).
- Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025 (31,000 workers, 31 countries): 46% of leaders say their companies use agents to automate workflows; 67% of leaders vs 40% of employees familiar with agents; 78% of leaders considering AI-specific hires — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born); [Microsoft blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/04/23/the-2025-annual-work-trend-index-the-frontier-firm-is-born/).
- Menlo Ventures 2025: enterprise gen-AI spend $37bn in 2025 (~3x in one year); survey ~500 US enterprise decision-makers — [press release via FinancialContent](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/gnwcq-2025-12-9-menlo-ventures-2025-state-of-generative-ai-report-enterprise-investment-hit-37b-in-2025-tripling-in-one-year); [Menlo PDF](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/menlo_ventures_enterprise_ai_report-2025.pdf) (not opened).

### Inferences
- The opportunity is not "adoption" (already done with general chatbots) but differentiation vs. ChatGPT/Copilot: consultants already use general tools daily, so the advisor must prove non-generic, context-specific insight and safe handling of client data.
- Client distrust of AI-generated deliverables suggests positioning as "sparring partner for the consultant's judgment" rather than "deliverable generator".

## 5. AI copilot pricing benchmarks and the Harvey analogy

### Takeaway
Horizontal assistants cluster at $20-30/user/month (premium seats ~$100); vertical research/professional copilots sell at $250-$1,500/seat/month with annual enterprise contracts (AlphaSense median ~$18k/yr; Hebbia ~$3k-10k/seat/yr; Harvey ~$1,200/lawyer/month list, 20-seat minimum). Harvey: ~$190m ARR (Jan 2026) → reportedly $300-400m+ by autumn 2026; valuation $11bn (Mar 2026) → ~$15.5bn (autumn 2026).

### Cited Findings
**Horizontal (list prices, mostly third-party summaries — check vendor pages)**
- Microsoft 365 Copilot: $30/user/month annual on top of a base licence; all-in ~$69 (E3) or ~$90 (E5) after July 2026 base-suite increases; Copilot Business (≤300 users) $21/user/month, promo $18 — [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/copilot-pricing); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/microsoft-365-pricing-increase-copilot-july-2026).
- ChatGPT Business: $20/user/month annual or $25 monthly (one source claims a $5 cut in April 2026 — unconfirmed); premium seat ~$100 annual/$125 monthly — [ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/copilot-vs-gemini-vs-chatgpt-vs-claude-business-pricing-2027/); [coworker.ai](https://coworker.ai/blog/enterprise-ai-price-index).
- Claude Team: Standard $20/seat/month annual ($25 monthly); Premium $100 annual ($125 monthly) — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/claude-alternatives-for-business-which-ai-plan-could-cut-costs/); [costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/claude-vs-microsoft-copilot/).

- Feb 2025: $300m Series D at $3bn valuation (Sequoia) — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/02/12/legal-ai-startup-harvey-300-million-series-d-funding-3-billion-valuation-sequoia/).
- ARR ~$100m (Aug 2025) → ~$190m (Jan 2026); March 2026: $200m at $11bn valuation led by Sequoia and GIC (prior round $8bn) — [implicator.ai](https://implicator.ai/harvey-raises-200-million-at-11-billion-as-vcs-chase-legal-ai-2/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/harvey/).
- Autumn 2026: $550m round at ~$15.5-16bn post-money; dates conflict (Aug 8 / Sep 9 / Oct 1, 2026); ARR reported at $300m (tracker) or $400m+ (press) — [SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/deals/harvey-raises-550m-in-funding-round-valuation-reaches-155b-fdaacbc6-e5ae-49f1-a9e7-224789dfde95); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/harvey-legal-ai-16b-valuation-funding/); [multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/harvey). Not verified against a company announcement.
- Legora (legal, Stockholm): Series D $550m (+$50m extension) at ~$5.55-5.6bn (Mar-Apr 2026); ~$100m revenue (Apr 2026); FT-reported talks at ≥$10bn — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/legora/); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/144827-legora-reportedly-in-talks-to-raise-at-a-10b-valuation/); [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/legora-funding-round-valuation-triple).

### Inferences
- Pricing ladder for the product: (a) prosumer independents $30-60/month (above ChatGPT/Claude $20 to signal specialisation, below $100 premium seats); (b) "pro" tier ~$100-150/month (matches premium seats of Claude/ChatGPT); (c) enterprise strategy teams $50-150k ACV for 20-100 seats with SSO/DPA/admin — between horizontal ($30) and research-grade vertical tools ($1-1.5k/month).
- Harvey's arc (vertical, premium, seat minimums, big-firm logos first) worked because legal has high-value repetitive document work and tolerance for premium prices; consulting strategy advice is less repetitive and more judgment-based, so the analogy supports premium vertical positioning but warns about proving measurable value per seat.

### Gaps
- Official list prices for ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude Enterprise, Gemini Enterprise not confirmed.
- No pricing found for any consulting-specific AI copilot.

## 6. Venture funding trends: vertical AI in professional services; investor views on "AI for consulting"

### Takeaway
Vertical AI is a hot category (Menlo: ~$3.5bn vertical AI enterprise spend in 2025, ~3x; healthcare and legal lead). Legal AI drew mega-rounds (Harvey, Legora). "AI for consulting" is earlier: small pre-seed/seed rounds (Riplo, Ascentra Labs), plus incumbents and labs moving in (McKinsey-AWS JV; reported Anthropic-backed enterprise AI services firm). Investor thesis: "services as software" — services spend is ~6x software spend.

## 7. Regulation and compliance (EU AI Act, GDPR, France CIF/AMF, enterprise procurement)

### Takeaway
The product is very likely NOT a high-risk AI system under the EU AI Act; main duties are Article 50 transparency (in force 2 Aug 2026, untouched by the Digital Omnibus) and general AI literacy. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) postponed high-risk rules to Dec 2027/Aug 2028. GDPR requires a processor DPA with consultants/enterprises and care with US LLM transfers. In France, strategy/industrial-strategy and M&A advice to companies is an unregulated "service connexe" — avoid personalised recommendations on specific financial instruments (CIF/investment-advice perimeter). Enterprise buyers expect SOC 2 Type II/ISO 27001, DPA, EU data residency and zero data retention.

### Cited Findings
**EU AI Act and Digital Omnibus**
- Provisional agreement 7 May 2026; Parliament vote 16 Jun 2026; Council final approval 29 Jun 2026; published in OJ 24 Jul 2026 as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744; entered into force 27 Jul 2026 — [Lewis Silkin](https://lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/09/council-of-the-eu-gives-ai-omnibus-final-green-light-102nbb1); [ECIJA](https://www.ecija.com/en/news-and-insights/the-european-parliament-and-the-council-approve-the-digital-omnibus-package-on-ai/); [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/); [EEAS/Council](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/chile/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-simplify-and-streamline-rules_en).
- New deadlines: Annex III standalone high-risk systems (education, employment/HR, credit scoring, critical infrastructure, law enforcement) from 2 Aug 2026 → 2 Dec 2027; Annex I product-embedded systems 2 Aug 2027 → 2 Aug 2028; sandbox deadline → 2 Aug 2027; prohibited-practice regime and GPAI rules not changed — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [IDTechWire](https://idtechwire.com/eu-moves-to-delay-high-risk-ai-act-compliance-to-december-2027/).
- New Article 5 bans (AI sexual deepfakes/CSAM) from 2 Dec 2026 — [Lewis Silkin](https://lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/09/council-of-the-eu-gives-ai-omnibus-final-green-light-102nbb1).
- Article 50 transparency applies from 2 Aug 2026, not deferred; only grace period: Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking for generative systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026 → 2 Dec 2026 (provider-side); deployer duties (Art. 50(4) deepfakes / AI-generated public-interest text) not covered by grace. One Mondaq piece claims Art. 50(1) also has a grace period — conflicting, likely wrong — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/new-technology/1843358/eu-ai-act-article-50); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/2026/08/102nfqm/you-talkin-to-me-operationalising-the-eu-ai-acts-transparency-obligations).

**GDPR / CNIL**

**Enterprise procurement**
- "83% of enterprise buyers require SOC 2" — repeated by vendor blogs without a named survey (unverified) — [Bright Defense](https://brightdefense.com/resources/soc-2-for-enterprise-clients). SOC 2 Type II described as "entry ticket for enterprise AI"; buyers increasingly ask for AI-specific testing and ISO 42001; AI security reviews reportedly take 4-8 weeks longer than non-AI ones (vendor blog) — [StackAI](https://www.stackai.com/insights/why-soc-2-type-ii-compliance-is-essential-for-enterprise-ai-platforms); [Software Secured](https://www.softwaresecured.com/post/why-enterprise-buyers-require-ai-pentests); [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/27/80-question-wall-enterprise-ai-security-questionnaire).

### Inferences
- AI Act role: by building and marketing its own AI system on top of a third-party model via API, the startup is the "provider" of an AI system (GPAI model obligations fall on OpenAI/Anthropic etc. unless the startup substantially modifies a model). Strategy advice to consultants is not an Annex III use case → not high-risk, as long as the tool is not used for HR decisions (e.g., evaluating/firing staff in a "transformation" plan) or creditworthiness. Practical duties: Art. 50(1) disclosure that users are interacting with AI (largely obvious in a branded AI side-panel, but state it), AI literacy measures, and contractual terms prohibiting high-risk uses.
- GDPR: for client documents read in the browser, the consultant/enterprise is controller; the startup is processor (Art. 28 DPA needed), LLM providers are sub-processors; offer EU processing (OpenAI EU residency or Bedrock/Vertex EU), ZDR, no training on customer data, and a sub-processor list. Background (not verified this session): US transfers typically rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
- France financial-advice safe zone: corporate strategy, industrial strategy, capital structure and M&A strategic advice to companies = unregulated ancillary service → OK. Avoid: personalised recommendations to buy/sell/hold specific financial instruments (shares, bonds, funds) for a client's investment — that is investment advice requiring CIF/PSI status. Add product guardrails and a disclaimer; avoid features like "which stocks to buy" or retail wealth advice.
- Enterprise go-to-market will require SOC 2 Type II (or ISO 27001 for EU buyers), SSO/SAML, DPA, EU data residency, ZDR, admin controls — budget 6-12 months to get SOC 2 Type II; start with independents (self-serve) while building compliance.

## 8. Chrome Web Store / browser extensions and B2B distribution

### Takeaway
Enterprise IT commonly runs default-deny extension policies (block all, allowlist approved IDs) via Chrome Enterprise/Group Policy/Intune; users can request extensions via an admin workflow. An extension-based product therefore needs an "IT-approval" path (force-install, admin docs, minimal permissions) for corporate teams, while independents can self-install. No statistic on the share of enterprises enforcing allowlists was found.

### Cited Findings
- ExtensionInstallAllowlist specifies approved extensions; ExtensionInstallBlocklist blocks and disables installed versions; common pattern: blocklist "*" then allowlist approved IDs (default-deny); ExtensionSettings policy takes precedence; old "Whitelist/Blacklist" names renamed — [Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/chrome-enterprise/how-to-manage-chrome-extensions-on-windows-and-mac?hl=en); [Fleet](https://fleetdm.com/guides/block-chrome-extensions); [Google Admin Help](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/6177431?hl=en-EN).
- Chrome Enterprise "extension workflows" let users request extensions which admins approve — [Google Admin Help](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/10405494?hl=en).
- Force-installed extensions cannot be blocked by the user-level blocklist (macOS vendor doc) — [swif.ai](https://help.swif.ai/en/articles/15419640-apple-google-chrome-extension-deployment-and-blocking-policy).
- Google guide "Getting started managing browser extensions" (Chrome Enterprise) — [Google PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/en_gb_getting_started_managing_browser_extensions_guide_chrome_enterprise.pdf).
- DISA STIG for Google Chrome includes an extension allowlist control (government-grade baseline) — [STIG Viewer](https://trial.stigviewer.com/stigs/google_chrome_current_windows/2025-05-15/finding/V-221563).

### Inferences
- An extension that reads the open document requests broad permissions (host permissions / active tab), which security teams scrutinise; minimising permissions (activeTab, on-click) and publishing a security whitepaper eases allowlisting.
- Many large consulting firms and corporates run Microsoft Edge (Chromium; supports Chrome Web Store extensions with its own policies) and M365 desktop apps; a side-panel extension only sees browser-based documents (Google Docs, Office for the web, web PDFs) — consider an Office add-in path later.
